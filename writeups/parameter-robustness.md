## The short answer

**The lock is not a knife edge that breaks when a number moves, but it is not a flat plateau everywhere either.**

- **Plateau:** the cold-streak size (x0.15 to x0.35 all land within $1.3k of each other in 2021-25 and within $0.5k in 2026),
  the late-entry size (x0.25 to x0.75: within $0.4k), and the trending-day breakeven (+3R, +3.5R, +4R, +5R and "never"
  are the same to within $130; below +3R the money drops step by step to the morning rule's +$11.5k).
- **Smooth scale, not an edge:** the $ risk ($600 to $1,000) and the wide-stop rule (full risk up to 15, 20, 25, 30, 40
  points or any stop). More risk means more net, more drawdown and more failed evals, in step. No setting is special.
- **One ridge: the cold-streak lookback.** The filter adds up the last 15 finished signals. At 15 or 16 the 5-year
  result is +$16.4k / +$16.9k; at 14 it is +$13.3k; at 13 or 12 it is +$7.8k; at 17 or 18 about +$10.7k. All of
  those still beat the morning rules (+$6.5k) and the filter switched off (+$4.7k), so the filter itself is robust; the
  exact number 15 is what makes 2021-25 look as good as it does. The honest 5-year figure is "+$8k to +$17k
  ($150 to $330 a month)", not "+$16.4k".
- **Two compromise settings, not peaks:** the 0R threshold and the 0.45 trending-day threshold. Moving either one step
  helps one period and hurts the other (0R: -1R gives 2026 +$23.0k but 2021-25 +$13.5k; +1R gives 2021-25 +$17.0k with a
  much smaller drawdown but 2026 +$17.4k). The lock sits where both periods are acceptable, not where either is best.
- **The whole neighbourhood is profitable.** 105 combinations of lookback 12-18, cold size x0.15-x0.35 and late size
  x0.35-x0.65: 2021-25 from +$6.8k to +$18.3k (median +$10.8k, every one above the morning rules), 2026 from +$19.6k to
  +$23.4k. The lock ranks 16th of 105 in 2021-25 and 85th of 105 in 2026 (its lookback costs one 2026 trade, see below).

What to take from it: keep the lock as it is (nothing here is clearly better in both periods), but read its 5-year
numbers as the top of a range. Rolling windows and the year table below show what the bad stretches look like.

**Reading it:**

- Up and down the columns the money moves a lot: 15-16 is a ridge (+$16.4k to +$18.3k), 14 is a step down, 10-13
  and 17-20 are a different world (+$6.3k to +$13.3k). Across the rows (the cold size) it barely moves.
- **Why.** Over 5 years the strategy makes about 0R before sizing (285R won, 287R lost). All of the 5-year money
  comes from which trades get shrunk. The lookback decides exactly when the filter switches to quarter size and back,
  and that timing matters most in 2023 and 2024: at N=15 the trades it shrinks in 2023 add up to +3.0R (it stays out of
  the way of that year's winners) and the ones it shrinks in 2024 add up to -14.3R (it catches that year's losers). At
  N=13 those figures are +11.7R and -9.5R; at N=17 they are +16.1R and -11.9R: both shrink a lot of 2023 winners.
  That is a few dozen trades going one way or the other over five years, not a law.
- **2026 is one trade.** At N=12-14 and 17-19 the filter also fires in 2026 (23-25 quarter-size trades) and the result
  is the same +$22,955 for all of them. N=15 gives +$20,487 because exactly one trade differs: the +5R winner on
  2026-08-07 is taken at 3 micros instead of 13 (-$2,468). Everything else in 2026 is identical.
- **Where the 15 came from.** It was picked on 2021-24 only, in the old "skip" mode, where neighbours 14-18 all
  improved every period. In the quarter-size mode that is now live, the ridge is narrower than that note suggests.
- **What it does not mean.** It does not mean the filter is a fluke: every N from 10 to 20 beats the filter switched off
  (2021-25 +$4,688 at the lock's other settings) and every N from 11 up beats the morning rules (+$6,491). It means
  the extra $6-9k that N=15-16 shows over its neighbours is partly the sample, and the 2021-25 result should be read as

limit: at $900 the 2021-25 1-month fail rate is 8.8% (lock 5.2%); at a 25-pt reference it is 9.9%; with the rule off it
is 19.8%. $700 and 15 pts cut the fails but drop setups (a few wide-stop trades fall under 1 micro) and money. The risk
research already drew this line; the point here is only that nothing happens abruptly anywhere on it.

## Plateau or peak, parameter by parameter

| parameter | lock | neighbours | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| cold-streak lookback | 15 | 16 same; 14 -$3k; 13 / 12 -$8.6k; 17 / 18 -$5.7k (2021-25). 2026: +$2.5k at every neighbour except 16 and 20 (one trade) | **ridge** (15-16): the only knife edge |
| cold-streak threshold | 0R | -1R: 2026 +$2.5k, 2021-25 -$2.9k; +1R: 2026 -$3.1k, 2021-25 +$0.6k with a smaller drawdown | compromise between the periods |
| cold size | x0.25 | x0.15-0.35 within $1.3k / $0.5k | plateau |
| late-entry size | x0.5 | x0.25-0.75 within $0.4k / $0.5k | plateau |
| breakeven on trending days | +3R | +3.5R to never: same money; +2.5R -$1.9k; +1R -$4.8k (2021-25) | plateau from +3R up (= never) |
| trending-day threshold | 0.45 | 0.40 -$1.4k; 0.50 +$1.2k with more drawdown and fails; 0.55 -$1.9k (2021-25) | noisy, keep |
| $ risk | $800 | $700 / $900: about -$2.3k / +$1.9k in 2021-25, fails 2.3% / 8.8% | smooth scale |
| full risk up to | 20 pts | 15 / 25: -$6.1k / +$2.1k in 2021-25, fails 3.0% / 9.9% | smooth scale |

**Overall: a plateau with one ridge.** The strategy's rules (entries, stops, targets, management) are not sensitive to
any number tested here, and the sizing rules are either flat or move smoothly. The one place where the 5-year result
depends on a single number is the cold-streak lookback, and that number also costs one big 2026 trade. Keep 15 (there is
no evidence that any other value is better out of sample; 16 is the same), but do not promise +$16k over 5 years: the
neighbourhood says +$8k to +$17k, with 2022 negative and a flat stretch of over a year in every version.

## How this was done, and how sure it is

- Every number is from the same exact live-loop replay the dashboard uses (768 trades, TradingView CME NQ1! 5-minute
  data, commissions and slippage included), re-sized or re-managed after the fact in ways that are exact: sizes are
  linear in the micros; exits were re-simulated with a line-by-line copy of the engine's exit block (it reproduces the
  lock on 768 of 768 trades and matched the full replay of +3.5R on 768 of 768); the two settings that change the
  engine's own micros (the $ risk and the wide-stop reference) use the engine's rule and, for the wide-stop reference,
  the engine's own replays of those settings. Two full engine replays were run (+3.5R breakeven, efficiency 0.50) and
  both agree with the fast methods.
- No look-ahead: every rule sees only the setup fields and the signals that had finished before entry.
- **Sample sizes:** 679 trades in 2021-25 is enough to see the direction of a sizing rule, not to tell a lookback of 15
  from 16 or 14. The 2026 differences between most neighbours are a single trade. The breakeven plateau above +3R rests
  on about 5 trades in 5 years. Read every difference under about $1k in 2021-25 and $500 in 2026 as "the same".
- The 1-month eval columns start a fresh MFFU Builder 50K eval on every trading day; they move with size, so they are
  the honest side of every "more money" row.
- Runs: every row of the first table is stored in the research library (ids above) with all its trades; the grids are in
  `research/archive/engine-2026-10-17/sensitivity.json` (moved 2026-10-20: research/lab/sensitivity.json is now LIT's, tools/lit_sensitivity.py); the code is `tools/research_sens.py`.
